## What furniture do you need for a 2BHK flat?

Start with what you will use every day, and leave the rest for later. The table splits each room into must-haves and pieces that can wait.

| Room | Must-have | Can wait |
|---|---|---|
| Entrance | Shoe rack or shoe cabinet | Shoe bench, mirror |
| Living room | Sofa, centre table, TV unit | Recliner, side tables, wall decor |
| Dining | Dining table and chairs | Crockery unit |
| Kitchen | Base and wall cabinets, counter top, steel baskets | Tall pantry unit, loft cabinets |
| Main bedroom | Bed, mattress, wardrobe, bedside tables | Dressing table, bedroom TV unit |
| Second bedroom | Bed, mattress, wardrobe | Study table, bookshelf |

A few notes on each room:

- **Entrance.** Shoes pile up from day one. A closed cabinet hides them. A wall-mounted flip-down unit also keeps the floor clear in a narrow passage.
- **Living room.** Measure the wall before you pick a sofa. A 3+2 set is the usual choice, and an L-shape uses a corner well. Plan the TV unit with the sofa, so the screen sits at a comfortable distance.
- **Dining.** Measure the dining area first. Leave room to pull the chairs out and walk behind them. If space is tight, a four-seater is easier to fit than a six-seater.
- **Kitchen.** This is the largest single piece of work. Our page on [modular kitchens in Kondhwa](/interiors/modular-kitchen) shows layouts and finishes.
- **Main bedroom.** A storage bed with a hydraulic lift gives you a second wardrobe under the mattress.
- **Second bedroom.** Think about who will use it. A sofa cum bed suits a study that hosts guests. A bunk bed with drawers suits two children.

## Which furniture should be custom-made and which ready-made?

Make it custom when it is fixed to a wall or has to fit an exact gap. Buy it ready-made when a standard size fits and you may want to move it later.

| Item | Custom-made | Ready-made | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kitchen cabinets | Yes | Rarely | Must fit the walls, sink and window |
| Wardrobes | Yes | For small rooms | Floor-to-ceiling storage needs exact sizes |
| TV unit | Usually | Sometimes | Planned around the wall and TV size |
| Shoe rack | Usually | Sometimes | Fits the space by the door |
| Sofa | Either | Either | Custom for an exact size, fabric or colour |
| Bed | Either | Either | Custom for storage or a special headboard |
| Mattress | No | Yes | Bought to the bed size |
| Chairs and small tables | Either | Often | Easy to buy and swap later |

## In what order should you furnish a new flat?

Do the work that hides inside walls and ceilings first, and bring in loose furniture last. This way nothing finished has to be broken open again.

1. **Measure and plan.** Mark where the bed, sofa, TV and dining table will go. This fixes where you need power points.
2. **Electrical and plumbing.** Add or move points for the TV, bedside lamps, kitchen appliances and water purifier.
3. **False ceiling.** If you want one, it goes in now, with its light points.
4. **Painting.** Most of the painting is done before fitting. Touch-ups follow at the end.
5. **Fixed units.** Kitchen, wardrobes, TV unit and shoe rack are fitted.
6. **Loose furniture.** Sofa, beds, mattresses and dining table come in.
7. **Curtains, lights and decor.** Measure curtains only after the ceiling and tracks are in place.

Some contractors paint after the woodwork. Agree the order with everyone before work begins.

## What drives the cost of furnishing a 2BHK?

Size and materials drive the cost more than style. Two flats with the same plan can end far apart.

- **Kitchen size and finish.** The length of cabinets, the shutter finish and the number of baskets.
- **Wardrobe area.** Floor-to-ceiling wardrobes with lofts use far more board than a standard height.
- **Board grade.** Grades that resist water cost more. The kitchen sink unit needs one most.
- **Shutter finish.** Laminate costs less than gloss or acrylic.
- **Hardware.** Soft-close hinges, drawer runners and hydraulic bed lifts.
- **Sofa size and fabric.** An L-shape with a chaise uses more frame, foam and fabric than a 3+2 set.
- **Extras.** False ceilings, lighting, and feature panels behind the TV.

## How can you save without regrets?

Spend first on what is hard to change, and save on what is easy to replace.

- Get the kitchen and wardrobes right first. Redoing them later means breaking finished work.
- Buy the must-haves now and add the can-wait pieces over the next year.
- Choose finishes for all rooms together, so colours match even if you buy in stages.
- Use standard mattress sizes, so a new mattress always fits.
- Skip the false ceiling in rooms where you do not need hidden lights.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the basic furniture cost for a 2BHK?

That covers a kitchen, two wardrobes, two beds, a sofa, a TV unit, a dining table and a shoe rack in laminate finishes. Mattresses, appliances and curtains are extra. Better board, soft-close hardware or gloss shutters move you into a higher tier.

### What furniture should I buy first for a new flat?
Buy what you need to live there from the first night: beds and mattresses, a wardrobe, kitchen cabinets, a shoe rack and somewhere to sit. Plan the kitchen and wardrobes early, because they are made to measure and fitted before the loose furniture. The dining table, crockery unit and decor can follow.

### Should I buy a furniture set for a 2BHK?
A set can be simple, but check what it contains. Some sets leave out the kitchen and wardrobes, which cost the most. Others use one size for every flat. Compare a set with a quote made for your own plan, piece by piece, with the board, finish and hardware named for each item.

### How do I choose furniture design for a 2BHK flat?
Start with space and storage, then pick the look. Sliding wardrobes suit tight bedrooms. A storage bed adds space under the mattress. A sofa cum bed lets the second bedroom work as a study and a guest room. Keep one colour and finish across rooms so the flat feels connected.
