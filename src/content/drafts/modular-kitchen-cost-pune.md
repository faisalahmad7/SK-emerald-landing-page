## How is modular kitchen cost worked out: per sq ft or per running foot?

Per sq ft means the area of the cabinets, not the floor. Per running foot means the length of cabinets along the wall. Both are fair, as long as you know which one a quote uses.

**Per square foot.** The maker multiplies the height by the width of each cabinet front and adds them up. For example, a run 10 ft long with cabinets 2.5 ft high has 25 sq ft of front. Some makers measure the whole box instead, which gives a bigger number. Ask which one they mean.

**Per running foot.** The maker measures the length of the run along the wall. Base units and wall units may be priced apart or together. This is simpler, but it hides height and depth. A tall wall unit and a short one can carry the same rate.

## How do the board and shutter finish change the price?

The board is the hidden cost. The shutter finish is the one you see. Together they make up most of the cabinet rate.

**Board.** Plain particle board and MDF cost the least, but they swell badly if water gets in. Moisture-resistant (MR) plywood costs more, and boiling-water-resistant (BWR) plywood more again. Boiling-water-proof (BWP) plywood, often sold as marine grade, costs the most of the three. HDHMR, a dense engineered board, is often offered too, so ask for its price next to plywood. ## Counter tops: what does granite or quartz add?

The counter top is usually priced apart from the cabinets, by the square foot or running foot of stone.

Granite is a natural stone. It handles hot pans well and is usually the cheaper of the two. It does need sealing from time to time, so oil and turmeric do not stain it. Quartz is an engineered stone made of crushed quartz bound with resin. It does not need sealing and resists stains. But very hot pans can mark it, so use a stand.

The edge shape, the cut-outs for the hob and sink, and any matching backsplash are also counted. They are small items that add up on a long run.

## Hardware, kitchen trolleys and accessories

This is where quotes differ most. Two kitchens with the same board and shutters can end far apart because of what goes inside.

- **Hinges.** Soft-close hinges stop doors from banging. They cost more than basic hinges.
- **Drawers.** Telescopic channels are simple runners. Tandem boxes are full drawer systems, often soft-close, and cost more per drawer.
- **Lift-ups.** These fittings let a wall shutter swing up and stay open.
- **Kitchen trolleys.** In Pune, this usually means steel pull-out baskets for plates, cups, cutlery and pots. Ask whether the steel is 304 or 202 grade. 304 resists rust better.
- **Corner and tall units.** Corner carousels, bottle pull-outs and tall pantry units are handy, but they are among the costlier pieces.

## How do you compare modular kitchen quotes?

Compare quotes only when they describe the same kitchen. A lower price for a weaker board is not a saving.

1. Give every maker the same measurements and the same layout.
2. Check if each rate is per sq ft or per running foot. If sq ft, is it shutter area or box area?
3. Line up the board grade, shutter finish and hardware side by side.
4. Count the baskets and accessories in each quote.
5. List what each quote leaves out.
6. Check whether GST and fitting are included.

## How can you keep the cost down?

Spend where water and daily use hit hardest, and save where they do not.

- Use the stronger board for the sink unit and a standard grade for dry units.
- Pick laminate for wall units and keep the gloss for the base run, or the other way round.
- Use deep drawers in place of many separate baskets.
- Keep the layout simple. Every corner and odd size adds cost.
- Decide everything before making starts. Late changes cost the most.
