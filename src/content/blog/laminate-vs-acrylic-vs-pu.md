Laminate is a tough, printed sheet that comes in matte, textured or gloss faces, and it is the easiest finish to live with. Acrylic is a mirror-like gloss sheet that looks rich but shows fine scratches and fingerprints. PU is paint sprayed on in layers, and veneer is a thin slice of real wood.

For a busy kitchen, matte or textured laminate is the low-worry choice. Gloss, PU and veneer suit people who like the look and don't mind a little more care. Below, we compare all four on looks, scratches, heat, cleaning and repair.

## What is the difference between laminate and acrylic?

Laminate is made from layers of paper soaked in resin and pressed hard under heat. Acrylic is a solid plastic sheet with a deep, glass-like shine. Both are glued onto a board, such as plywood, MDF or HDHMR.

**Laminate** comes in plain colours, wood grains, stone looks and textures. It can be matte or gloss. It stands up well to scratches and daily heat, and a damp cloth cleans it. Its weak point is the edge. Standard laminate shows a thin dark line where it is cut, and the raw edge of the board needs covering too. So every edge gets an edge band. If water gets under that band, the edge can lift.

**Acrylic** gives a high-gloss face in bright or deep colours. It looks rich and is easy to wipe. But fine scratches and fingerprints show, more so under kitchen lights. Light scratches can sometimes be polished out with a special polish.

So the real choice is between a hard-wearing surface and a glossy one that asks for regular wiping.

## Laminate vs sunmica: is there a difference?

No. Sunmica is a brand name for laminate. It has been sold in India for so long that people say "sunmica" for any decorative laminate, the way "Xerox" is used for a photocopy.

When a carpenter offers "sunmica", they mean laminate. The useful question is not laminate or sunmica. It is which laminate sheet, in what finish, and from which range.

## Glossy or matte laminate: which is best?

Matte suits kitchens and children's rooms. Gloss suits spaces where you want shine and don't mind wiping.

Gloss laminate bounces light and can make a small room feel brighter. It also shows smudges and fine scratches. Matte and textured sheets hide fingerprints and everyday marks far better. Some matte ranges are sold as anti-fingerprint. Wood-grain textures are forgiving too, because small marks blend into the pattern.

## Laminate vs PU finish

PU gives a smooth painted look with no joint lines, in almost any colour. It costs more, takes longer to make and is harder to repair at home.

PU stands for polyurethane, a type of paint. It is sprayed onto MDF in several coats, with sanding between coats, and each coat is left to cure. Because it is paint, it covers curves and grooves where a flat laminate sheet can't easily go. It comes in matte or gloss. A chip or a deep scratch needs a painter to touch it up or respray the shutter.

## Laminate vs veneer

Veneer is a thin slice of real wood, so each shutter has its own grain. A wood-grain laminate is a printed copy of that look.

Veneer is glued onto board and then polished. It looks and feels like solid wood. Water, steam and strong sun are its enemies. Spills must be wiped at once, sun can fade it, and it may need polishing again over the years. It suits wardrobes and TV units away from windows better than shutters near a sink or hob.

## Which finish is best for kitchens and wardrobes?

Kitchens need a tough, wipeable face. Wardrobes can take softer, glossier finishes.

**In the kitchen**, shutters near the hob face heat and oil, and the ones under the sink face water. Laminate, especially matte, is the safe pick for these. Acrylic and PU look good on wall cabinets and tall units, where they catch the eye and see less wear. Whatever the finish, ask for every edge to be sealed, even the hidden ones. Our [modular kitchen page](/interiors/modular-kitchen) shows how we plan cabinets and finishes together.

**In the bedroom**, a wardrobe gets handled, not splashed. Gloss, acrylic, PU and veneer all do well here. A two-tone mix is common, such as a gloss colour with a wood-grain laminate. Our [wardrobes](/collections/wardrobes) come in gloss, matte and laminate finishes.

**On a TV unit**, you can be bolder. It is seen more than it is touched, so a strong colour or texture works well.

To choose a colour, look at a large sample, not a small chip. Then see it in daylight, because shop lights change how colours look.

## How to look after your shutters

Gentle cleaning keeps any finish looking new. Harsh pads and strong cleaners are the main risk.

- Wipe gloss shutters with a dry microfibre cloth. Avoid abrasive pads and harsh cleaners.
- Wipe laminate with a slightly damp soft cloth and dry it straight away.
- For PU and veneer, use a soft cloth and wipe spills at once. Keep veneer out of strong direct sun.
- Don't hang wet towels over shutter edges. Over time, water can creep under the edge band.

## What to ask for in your quotation

A good quotation names the finish on every shutter, not just "laminate".

- The finish on each shutter: laminate, acrylic, PU or veneer
- Gloss, matte or textured, and the shade name or code
- What goes on the inside face. A sheet on both sides helps stop a shutter from bending.
- Edge banding: matched in colour and sealed on all edges
- The board underneath each shutter
- The handles and hinges, and whether the hinges are soft-close

## Frequently asked questions

### Are sunmica and laminate the same?

Yes, in everyday use. Sunmica is a brand of decorative laminate. The name became so common in India that people use it for any laminate sheet. So "sunmica vs laminate" is not a real choice. What matters is the sheet itself: its finish, its texture, and how well its edges are sealed when it is fixed to the board.

### Which is better for a kitchen, laminate or acrylic?

For base cabinets near the hob and sink, laminate is usually better. It resists scratches and heat well and hides marks, especially in matte. Acrylic gives a deep gloss that many people love, but it shows fingerprints and fine scratches. A common mix is laminate on the base cabinets and acrylic or gloss on the wall cabinets.

### Which laminate is best, glossy or matte?

It depends on the room and on how often you clean. Matte and textured laminates hide fingerprints and small scratches, so they suit kitchens and children's rooms. Gloss laminate reflects light and looks bright, but it shows smudges. For a wardrobe, either works well. Many people mix the two, with gloss on some panels and matte on the rest.

### What is the difference between laminate and veneer?

Laminate is a pressed sheet with a printed or plain face, so it is tough, even and easy to clean. Veneer is a thin slice of real wood, so the grain is natural and every panel is different. Veneer needs polishing and protection from water and sun. Laminate needs very little care beyond a quick wipe.

### Which laminate is best for a wardrobe?

Any good decorative laminate works, because a wardrobe sees little water or heat. Choose by look and by how much wiping you want to do. Gloss looks bright but shows prints, while matte and wood-grain hide them. Ask for the same sheet on both faces of each door, and for edges that are colour-matched and sealed.
