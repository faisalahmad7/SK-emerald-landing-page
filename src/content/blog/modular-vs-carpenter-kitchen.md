A modular kitchen is built as boxes in a factory and then fitted in your flat. A carpenter-made kitchen is built by hand, inside your flat. Modular usually means neater edges, less dust and easier repairs. Carpenter work can bend to any shape and may cost less, but the finish depends on the person doing it. This guide compares both, plus the semi-modular middle path, so you can pick the one that suits your home.

## What is the difference between a modular and a non-modular kitchen?

The difference is where the cabinets are made. In a modular kitchen, each cabinet is a complete box. The panels are cut by machine and the edges are sealed. The boxes then come to your home. There they are screwed together, fixed to the wall and topped with the counter.

In a carpenter-made kitchen, the carpenter brings sheets of board to your flat. He measures, cuts, glues and nails it all there. The cabinets are often built against the walls, and a wall or the platform may act as one side. The result is a fixed piece of joinery, not a set of boxes.

**Semi-modular** sits in between, and people use the word in two ways. Most often it means you keep the builder's concrete platform with its granite top. Shutters, drawers and baskets are fitted under it, with wall cabinets above. Some people also call a carpenter-built kitchen with factory-made shutters semi-modular. Ask which one a quote means.

| | Modular | Semi-modular | Carpenter-made |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where it is made | Boxes made in a factory, fitted on site | Builder's platform stays; units go under and above it | Built by hand in your flat |
| Edges and finish | Cut and edged by machine, even | Depends on who makes the units | Depends on the carpenter's skill |
| Work inside your flat | Mostly fitting | Less, as the platform is not broken | Most; all cutting happens on site |
| Dust and noise | Low | Medium | High |
| Repairs and changes | Swap a shutter, drawer or whole box | Units can change; the platform cannot | Fixed to the walls; changes mean cutting |
| Moving house | Boxes can go with you if sizes fit | Only the units, if they fit | Usually stays behind |
| First price | Often higher | Saves the cost of breaking the platform | Often lower, but can drift |

## What comes in a modular kitchen?

A modular kitchen has five parts: the carcass, the shutters, the hardware, the counter top and the accessories. Knowing them helps you read any quote, modular or not.

- **Carcass.** This is the box itself, made of board or plywood. It faces water from the sink and steam from cooking, so its grade matters most. Our guide to [BWP, BWR, MR and HDHMR boards](/blog/bwp-vs-bwr-vs-mr-plywood) explains the grades in plain words.
- **Shutters.** These are the doors and drawer fronts you see. They come in laminate, gloss, acrylic, PU paint or glass. See [laminate, acrylic and PU shutters compared](/blog/laminate-vs-acrylic-vs-pu) for how they wear.
- **Hardware.** Hinges, drawer runners, handles and lift-up fittings for wall cabinets. Soft-close hinges and runners stop doors and drawers from banging.
- **Counter top.** Usually granite or quartz. It is cut to the length of the cabinets, with holes for the hob and sink.
- **Accessories.** Steel baskets for plates, cups and cutlery, which many people in Pune call kitchen trolleys. Also corner units, bottle pull-outs and tall pantry units.

Most quotes leave out the sink, tap, chimney and hob. Wall tiles, plumbing changes and new electrical points are often extra too. Check this before you compare two prices.

## Is a modular kitchen good or bad for quality?

A modular kitchen is good when the board, edges and hardware are good. The word modular alone does not promise quality.

The main gain is at the edges. Board soaks up water through its cut edges, and wet edges swell. In a factory, a machine glues edge tape around each panel with heat and pressure, then trims it clean. A carpenter on site usually sticks the tape by hand. A skilled one does it well, but it is slower and less even. And the kitchen is the wettest room in the house.

Machine-cut panels are also square. Shutters line up and the gaps between them stay even.

Still, a modular kitchen with cheap board under the sink will fail. A careful carpenter using good plywood can build a kitchen that outlasts it. Judge the materials, not the label.

## How long does each one take, and how much mess?

A modular kitchen is mostly made away from your home. Once the boxes arrive, fitting is mainly assembly, so you lose the use of your kitchen for a shorter spell.

A carpenter-made kitchen is built where you live. Expect weeks of sawing, sanding, glue smell and sawdust. Your neighbours will hear it too, which matters in a housing society.

Time on site also means someone must supervise. They have to check the work each day, answer questions and keep track of materials. If you work long hours or live far from the flat, that is a real cost.

Semi-modular cuts some of the mess because the platform stays. There is no breaking of granite or tiles.

The total time depends on the size, the finish and how fast you decide. Ask for the timeline in writing before work starts.

## What about repairs and changes later?

Modular wins on repairs. Each unit is its own box. A swollen shutter, a broken drawer or even a whole cabinet can be replaced on its own. If you move house, the boxes can come with you, as long as the new kitchen has room for them. The counter top is cut to fit, so it usually stays behind.

A carpenter-made kitchen is fixed to the walls. Small repairs are easy if your carpenter comes back. Bigger changes, like adding a tall unit, mean cutting into finished work and matching the old laminate.

With semi-modular, you can change the shutters and baskets freely. The platform stays as it is unless you break it.

## Is a modular kitchen more expensive than a carpenter kitchen?

Often it looks that way at first. But a carpenter quote may leave out things a modular quote includes, and costs can drift as the work goes on. The fair way is to compare the same list of materials.

The things that drive cost are the same for all three types:

- **Size and layout.** More length of cabinets, more corners and a loft all add cost.
- **Board grade.** Grades that stand up to water cost more, and the sink unit needs one most.
- **Shutter finish.** Plain laminate costs less than gloss, acrylic or PU paint.
- **Hardware.** Soft-close hinges, drawer systems and lift-up fittings cost more than basic ones.
- **Accessories.** Every basket, corner unit and pull-out adds to the total.
- **Counter top.** The stone, its thickness and the edge shape.
- **Site work.** Breaking an old platform, plumbing, wiring and tiling.

## Which kitchen suits you?

Pick the type that fits your flat and your time, then spend on the materials.

| Choose | If you |
|---|---|
| Modular | Have a new flat, want an even finish, cannot supervise daily, or may move house |
| Semi-modular | Have a builder's platform in good shape and do not want to break it |
| Carpenter-made | Have an odd-shaped kitchen, want carved or traditional detail, and trust a carpenter you can supervise |

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a modular kitchen good or bad?
It is good when the materials are good. Factory-cut panels with machine-sealed edges stay square, and their edges resist water better than hand-taped ones. But a modular kitchen built on weak board, or with cheap hinges, will still sag and swell. Ask for the board grade, edge tape and hardware to be named in writing before you decide.

### What is the difference between a modular and a semi-modular kitchen?
A modular kitchen is made fully of factory-made boxes that can be taken apart. A semi-modular kitchen keeps the builder's concrete platform and granite top. Shutters, drawers and baskets are fitted under it, with wall cabinets above. Semi-modular saves the cost of breaking the platform. The trade-off is that the platform cannot be moved or changed later.

### What does a modular kitchen include?
It includes base cabinets, wall cabinets and sometimes tall units, each with shutters and hardware. Most quotes also add a counter top and a set of baskets or pull-outs. The sink, tap, chimney, hob, tiles, plumbing and electrical work are often extra. Read the list line by line and ask what has been left out.

### Which modular kitchen is best?
The best one has the right board, shutter finish and hardware for the way you cook. Use a board that stands up to water for the sink unit. Pick a shutter finish you can wipe clean easily. Choose hinges and runners made for daily use. A brand name matters less than the specification written into your quote.

### How do I set up a non-modular kitchen?
Start with what you have. If the builder's platform is sound, fit shutters and steel baskets under it. Add wall cabinets or open shelves above. Keep heavy pots low and daily items within easy reach. A carpenter can build the units on site, or a workshop can make them to your measurements and fit them.
