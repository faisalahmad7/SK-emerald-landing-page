A false ceiling is a second ceiling hung below the concrete slab, and POP is one of the materials used to make it. So the real question is which material to use. For most living rooms and bedrooms, gypsum board is the faster, cleaner choice. POP suits curves and carved detail, and PVC suits bathrooms and balconies.

Below, we compare each material, explain what drives the cost, and list what to ask for in your quotation.

## What is the difference between a false ceiling and POP?

A false ceiling is the structure. POP, short for plaster of Paris, is one way of building it.

A false ceiling hangs a few inches below the slab on a light metal frame. The gap hides wiring, holds recessed lights, and lets you shape coves and steps. The ceiling itself can be gypsum board, POP, PVC, mineral fibre tiles, wood or metal.

## Which false ceiling is best: POP or gypsum?

For a flat or gently stepped design, gypsum board is usually the better choice. For curves, domes and carved borders, POP gives more freedom.

Both start as gypsum, a soft mineral. The difference is how they reach your ceiling.

**Gypsum board** arrives as ready-made sheets with a gypsum core and a paper face. The sheets are screwed to a frame of galvanised steel channels. The joints are taped, filled and sanded, and then the whole ceiling is painted. The work is quick and fairly clean, and the finish is even.

**POP** is gypsum that has been heated and ground into powder. Mixed with water, it sets hard. On a ceiling it is applied by hand over a metal frame and then left to dry. A skilled worker can shape curves, cornices and carved designs that are hard to make from flat boards. But it is slower, dustier and wetter work, and the result depends a lot on the worker.

**Cracks** can happen with both. Gypsum ceilings tend to crack at the joints if the frame moves or the taping is rushed. POP cracks when the mix is wrong or the drying is hurried. Good framing and careful work matter more than the material.

## Which false ceiling is best: POP or PVC? And what about grid and wood?

PVC wins in wet rooms. Grid ceilings suit offices more than homes. Wood brings warmth but needs the most care.

**PVC panels** are plastic strips that lock together on a frame. They do not soak up water, so they suit bathrooms, balconies and utility areas. They are light and very quick to fit. The look is plainer, with visible panel lines. PVC softens with strong heat, and if it burns it melts and gives off harmful fumes, so keep it away from the hob.

**Grid ceilings** use square tiles, often 2 ft by 2 ft, dropped into a light metal grid. This is the common office ceiling. You can lift out any tile to reach the wiring or AC ducts above. Mineral fibre tiles soak up sound, but standard tiles can sag in damp air. They suit dry, air-conditioned rooms better than a humid flat.

**Wooden ceilings** use planks, veneered panels or slats. Often the wood is just a panel or strip set inside a gypsum ceiling. Wood can attract termites, so it must be treated, and it reacts to damp.

## Which false ceiling is best for a bathroom or kitchen?

Use PVC, or moisture-resistant gypsum board kept away from direct water.

A bathroom faces hot steam every morning and damp air all through the monsoon. PVC shrugs off water. Moisture-resistant gypsum board, often green in colour, copes with steam on a bathroom or kitchen ceiling. It should not sit right above a shower, though. Plain gypsum and POP take up moisture, so keep them out of wet rooms.

In any wet room, leave a small access panel. You will need it to reach the geyser, the exhaust fan and the pipes.

## Cove lights, spotlights and profile lights

In most homes, a false ceiling is mainly about light. Plan the lights first, then the shape.

- **Cove lighting** hides LED strips in a recessed edge. The light washes across the ceiling, with no glare in your eyes.
- **Spotlights** sit flush in the board and light a sofa, a wall or a dining table.
- **Profile lights** are aluminium channels holding an LED strip behind a cover. They give a clean line of light. They can be set into a gypsum ceiling, or fixed on the surface where there is no false ceiling at all.

Mark the fan, AC, chandelier and switch points before the frame goes up. Moving them later means cutting the ceiling.

## How much ceiling height will you lose?

Usually a few inches at the edges, and more for layered designs.

A simple cove along the walls needs only a shallow drop. Stepped and layered designs need more. Where recessed lights sit, the gap often needs to be around 4 inches (about 100 mm) or more, so the strip or fitting stays hidden. In a flat with a low slab, keep the centre of the room at full height and drop only the edges. That keeps the room from feeling squashed. Ask for the drop to be marked on the drawing.

## Which false ceiling is termite proof?

The gypsum, POP and PVC themselves are not termite food. Wood is, and so is the paper face on gypsum board.

Gypsum and POP are minerals, and PVC is plastic, so termites can't live on them. But gypsum board has a paper face, and termites that are already in a wall will eat paper. Wooden ceilings need treatment against termites and borers. If your building has a termite problem, treat it before the ceiling goes up.

## What drives the cost of a false ceiling?

Material, design, lights and site conditions set the price, not just the area.

- **Material.** PVC and plain gypsum board are usually at the lower end, and wood at the top. POP varies with how much hand detail it has.
- **Design.** A flat ceiling with one cove costs less than several layers, curves or a grid of recessed panels.
- **Lights.** Every metre of LED strip, every spotlight, driver and profile channel adds cost, and so does the electrician's time.
- **Board type.** Moisture-resistant board costs more than standard board.
- **Height and access.** Tall rooms need scaffolding.
- **Finish.** More coats of putty and paint give a better surface but take more time.
- **Size of the job.** A very small room can cost more per square foot, because the setup work is the same.

When you compare quotations, compare them line by line, not just by the rate per square foot.

## What to ask for in your quotation

A clear quotation lets you check the ceiling against what was promised.

- The ceiling material, and whether the board is standard or moisture-resistant
- The frame: what it is made of and how it is fixed to the slab
- A drawing with the drop at each point
- The number and type of lights, and warm or cool white for the strips
- Fan, AC and access panel positions
- Jointing, putty and paint coats, and the paint colour
- Who covers the floor and furniture, and who cleans up

## Frequently asked questions

### Which false ceiling is best, POP or gypsum?

For most flats, gypsum board. It goes up faster, makes less mess and gives an even, painted finish. POP is better when you want curves, domes or carved borders, because it is shaped by hand on site. Both can crack if the work is rushed, so the skill of the team matters as much as the material.

### Which false ceiling is best for a bathroom?

PVC is the safest choice, because it does not soak up water and won't sag in steam. Moisture-resistant gypsum board is another option, as long as it is not right above the shower. Avoid plain gypsum and POP in bathrooms. Whatever you choose, leave an access panel so the geyser, fan and pipes can be reached.

### Which false ceiling is cheapest?

PVC and a plain gypsum board ceiling are usually at the lower end. But the material is only part of the price. Layers, curves, coves and every light you add can cost more than the board itself. Moisture-resistant board, tall rooms and extra paint coats also add cost.

### What is the difference between a false ceiling and a profile light?

A false ceiling is a second ceiling hung below the slab. A profile light is a lighting fitting: an aluminium channel with an LED strip and a cover that gives a clean line of light. Profile lights are often set into a false ceiling, but surface-mounted ones can be fixed straight onto a plain ceiling or wall.

### False ceiling or fall ceiling: which is correct?

False ceiling is the correct term. "Fall ceiling" is a common mishearing of it, though people use both to mean the same thing. You may also see drop ceiling, suspended ceiling or down ceiling. They all describe a second ceiling hung on a frame below the real one, used to hide wiring and hold lights.
