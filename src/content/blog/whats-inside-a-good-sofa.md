To choose sofa material, start with the parts you cannot see. A good sofa has a dry, well-joined wooden frame, dense seat foam and a firm base of springs or webbing. Then pick a fabric made for daily use. Colour and leg style come last. Below is each part, how to check it in a showroom, and what to get written into your quotation.

## How to choose sofa material: the five parts that matter

Five parts decide how a sofa feels and how long it lasts. The frame holds the shape. The seat base and foam decide comfort. The fabric takes the daily wear. The legs carry the weight to the floor.

| Part | What it does | What good looks like | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frame | Holds the shape and takes the load | Seasoned, treated wood, glued and screwed, with corner blocks | Creaks, wobbles, or joints held only by staples |
| Seat base | Holds up the foam | Steel zig-zag springs, firm webbing or a solid board | Straps that sag when you press them |
| Foam | Gives comfort and shape | Dense seat foam, often layered | Soft in the shop, but no bounce |
| Fabric | Takes rubbing, sun and spills | A stated rub count for daily use | No fabric details given |
| Legs | Carry the weight to the floor | Bolted into a plate in the frame | Legs that turn loose or rock |

## Which sofa frame lasts longest?

A frame of dry, seasoned wood, with strong joints and braced corners, lasts longest. It is also the one part you cannot fix cheaply later.

**Seasoned wood.** Fresh wood still holds water. As it dries in your home, it shrinks. Joints loosen, the frame starts to creak, and small cracks open. Seasoned or kiln-dried wood has already lost most of that water, so it stays steady. Ask which wood is used and whether it has been seasoned.

**Treatment.** Pune's monsoon brings months of damp air. Ask whether the wood is treated against termites and borers. It is a small step at the making stage, and it protects the frame for years.

**Plywood and board.** Good plywood works well for some parts, such as the arm and back panels. Particle board and thin MDF are weaker. They should not carry the main load of the seat.

**Joints.** The best frames are glued and screwed, or glued and dowelled. Small wooden blocks are fixed into the corners to stop them twisting. Staples are fine for holding fabric in place. On their own, they are not enough to hold a frame together.

**Metal frames.** Some modern designs use a steel frame. Ask how it is protected from rust, as many flats get damp in the rains.

## How to choose sofa foam

Choose seat foam by its type and density, not by how soft it feels in the first minute. Soft foam feels lovely in a showroom. Cheap soft foam can also go flat within a year or two.

**Density** is the weight of the foam in one cubic metre. It is written as a number such as 32 or 40, in kg per cubic metre. Denser foam usually keeps its shape for longer. Many Indian buying guides suggest seat foam of 32 or more for a sofa you use every day. Back cushions can be softer.

**Density is not firmness.** Two foams of the same density can feel quite different. So sit on the sofa, and also ask for the density in writing.

| Foam or filling | How it feels | Where it works | Keep in mind |
|---|---|---|---|
| PU foam | Soft to medium | Everyday seats and backs | Quality varies, so ask for the density |
| HR (high resilience) foam | Springy, bounces back fast | Seats used every day | A grade of PU foam; usually costs more |
| Rebonded foam | Very firm | A base layer under softer foam | Too hard to sit on by itself |
| Memory foam | Slow, moulds to your body | A thin top layer | Can feel warm and slow to recover |
| Fibre fill | Soft and loose | Back and scatter cushions | Flattens and needs plumping |

Many good seats use layers. A firm base sits under a softer top layer. The foam is then wrapped in a thin sheet of fibre. This softens the edges and helps the fabric sit smoothly.

## Springs, webbing or a board: what holds the seat up?

The seat base sits under the foam, and it matters as much as the foam does. A weak base lets even good foam sink.

- **Zig-zag springs** are steel wires that run across the seat and clip to the frame. They give a springy seat. They last well when the wire is thick and firmly fixed.
- **Elastic webbing** is a set of strong rubber straps stretched across the frame. Good webbing is comfortable. Cheap webbing can stretch and sag over time.
- **A solid board base** does not sag, but it feels hard. It needs thicker, better foam on top.
Ask which base your sofa has. If it is webbing, ask how close the straps are.

## How to choose sofa fabric that lasts

Pick fabric by how much rubbing it can take, then by colour and feel. A sofa fabric gets rubbed each time someone sits down or slides back.

Fabric makers measure this with a rub test, often called the Martindale test. A machine rubs the fabric until it shows wear. The result is given as a number of rubs. Many suppliers suggest 15,000 to 25,000 rubs for an everyday family sofa. For homes with children, pets or recliners, look for 25,000 or more. Not every fabric book lists this number, so ask for it.

A few more points help:

- **Velvet** looks rich and feels soft. It can show light and dark patches where the pile gets pressed. That is normal for velvet, not a fault.
- **Tight weaves** stand up to pet claws better than loose, loopy ones.
- **Mid tones and textures** hide everyday marks better than very pale or very dark plain colours.
- **Sun** fades most fabrics. Keep the sofa out of strong, direct sun from a window.

## Sofa legs: how they are fixed matters

A leg should screw into a metal plate or a threaded insert fixed to the frame, not only into a thin bottom board. Slim metal legs look light and neat, but they still need a solid fixing above them. In the showroom, push the sofa gently from the side. It should not rock or twist on its legs.

## How to check a sofa in the showroom

Sit, press, lift and listen. Ten minutes in a showroom tells you more than any photo can.

| Check | How to do it | Good sign |
|---|---|---|
| Sit for five minutes | Sit the way you do at home | Feet flat, lower back supported |
| Press the front edge | Push down on the front of the seat | Firm, with no hard bar |
| Lift one corner | Raise one front corner a few centimetres | The other front leg lifts too: the frame is rigid |
| Listen | Shift your weight and lean back | No creaks or clicks |
| Press the arms | Push down hard on each arm | No wobble or sharp frame edge |
| Ask about the inside | Ask about the frame, base and foam | Clear answers before you pay |

## What to ask for in your quotation

Ask for each hidden part to be named in writing. Then the sofa you receive is the sofa you agreed to.

- Frame: the wood or board used, and whether it is seasoned and treated
- Seat base: springs, webbing or a board
- Seat foam: the type and the density
- Back cushions: foam or fibre fill
- Fabric: the name or code, the colour, and the rub count if listed
- Size: overall width and depth, plus seat height and seat depth
- Legs: what they are made of, their finish and their height
- Delivery and fitting: what is included, and whether your area is covered
- Warranty: whether there is one, and what it covers, in writing

A maker who knows their sofa will not mind writing this down.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I choose sofa foam?
Start with the seat. Ask for the foam type and its density, written as a number in kg per cubic metre. Denser foam usually lasts longer, and many Indian guides suggest 32 or more for seats used daily. Then sit on the sofa for a few minutes. A good seat feels supportive, springs back when you stand, and leaves no dip.

### How do I choose sofa fabric for a family home?
Look for a stated rub count. Many suppliers suggest 15,000 to 25,000 rubs for everyday family use, and 25,000 or more for homes with children or pets. Choose a tight weave if you have a cat or dog. Mid tones and textured fabrics hide marks well. Before you clean any fabric, ask the maker for care advice that suits it.

### How do I choose sofa colour for the living room?
Start with what stays in the room: the floor, the walls and any large wooden furniture. A mid-tone sofa in grey, beige, olive or blue suits most rooms and hides everyday marks. Add brighter colour with cushions, which are easy to change. Look at fabric samples in daylight at home, as showroom lights can change how a colour looks.

### How do I choose a sofa for a small living room?
Measure the wall, and leave room to walk past. A 3+2 set or a compact L-shape may suit better than a large 3+2+1. Slim arms and raised legs make a sofa look lighter. Check the seat depth too, because a deep seat takes up floor space. Ask for the sofa to be made to your measurements.

### What is the difference between a leather and a fabric sofa?
Real leather is animal hide. It wipes clean and lasts well with care, but it costs more and can feel hot in summer. Leatherette is a man-made coating on a fabric backing. It wipes clean too, but low-grade leatherette can peel in heat and damp. Fabric breathes better and comes in more colours. It needs gentle, regular cleaning.
