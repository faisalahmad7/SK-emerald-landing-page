Choose a recliner over a normal sofa if you want to lie back with your feet up, and you have room for it to open. Choose a manual recliner for simple use with no wiring. Choose power if you want smooth control at the press of a button, or if someone at home finds a lever hard to pull.

Below are the main types, how much space each needs, and how to measure your flat before you buy.

## Recliner vs sofa: which suits your living room?

A recliner gives deeper rest. A normal sofa seats more people in less space. Some homes do best with one of each: a sofa for guests and a recliner for the person who wants to rest.

| | Recliner | Normal sofa |
|---|---|---|
| Comfort | Back goes down and footrest comes up, for long rest | Upright seat, for talking and short sitting |
| Space | Needs room behind and in front to open | Sits close to the wall |
| Seats | Each reclining seat is wide | More seats along the same wall |
| Weight | Heavy, with a steel mechanism inside | Lighter and easier to move |
| Upkeep | Moving parts need care | Only frame, foam and fabric |
| Price | Usually more per seat, because of the mechanism | Usually less per seat |

Pick a recliner if someone at home watches long films, stands all day at work, or needs a seat to rest in. Pick a sofa if you have guests often or the room is narrow.

## Manual or power recliner: what's the difference?

A manual recliner opens with your body or a lever. A power recliner opens with a motor and a button. Both can be comfortable. The right one depends on who will use it.

| Type | How it opens | Good for | Keep in mind |
|---|---|---|---|
| Push-back | Lean back firmly and the footrest rises | A clean look with no handle | Needs some body strength |
| Lever or pull-tab | Pull a handle on the side, then lean back | Simple, reliable use | The lever needs a firm pull |
| Power | Press a button; it moves smoothly and stops at any angle | Easy control for anyone | Needs a power socket close by |
| Power-lift | A motor reclines the chair and also lifts it to help you stand | Elders, or anyone with weak knees or hips | Needs a socket; larger and heavier |

Push-back recliners can be hard for small children and some older people to open and close. A lever is easier, but it still needs a good grip.

Power recliners need one extra question. Ask whether the chair has a battery backup. Without one, a power recliner may stay where it stopped during a power cut, until the power comes back.

## How much space does a recliner need?

A standard recliner often needs 25 to 60 cm behind it to open. A wall-hugger, also called a zero-wall recliner, often needs 15 cm or less. The exact figure depends on the model, so check the one you plan to buy.

A wall-hugger works because its seat slides forward as the back goes down. The chair moves into the room instead of into the wall. That makes it a good choice for a flat where the recliner must sit close to a wall.

The footrest also needs space in front. Open the recliner fully in the showroom. Measure from the back of the chair to the tip of the footrest. That is the floor length you need, plus room to walk past.

### How to measure your flat for a recliner

Take a tape measure and note down:

- The wall length where the recliner will sit
- The floor depth from that wall to the nearest table or walkway
- Space to walk past when the footrest is up
- The nearest power socket, if you choose a power recliner
- The width of your main door, your lift, and any tight corners

Share these numbers before you order. At SK Emerald Interior, we ask for the wall length and the floor depth, so the recliner can be planned to open safely. Recliners are heavy, so also ask whether the back lifts off for delivery. This helps in small lifts and narrow stairs.

## Swivel-glider and rocking recliners

A swivel-glider recliner turns on its base and glides gently back and forth. It also reclines. A rocking recliner rocks like an old-style rocking chair. Both suit a reading corner, or a parent settling a baby. They need clear space around them, because the chair moves even when it is upright.

## Power-lift recliners for elderly parents

A power-lift recliner helps someone stand up. A motor raises the whole chair and tilts it forward. The user's feet reach the floor, and they can rise with less strain on the knees. It also reclines like a normal power recliner.

Check these points before you buy one:

- The hand control should have large, clear buttons.
- The seat height and depth should suit the user. Their feet should rest flat on the floor when the chair is upright.
- Ask about a battery backup, so the chair can return upright in a power cut.
- Let the person who will use it try it first.

If the user has a health condition, ask their doctor what kind of seat suits them.

## Single recliner or a recliner sofa set?

A single recliner suits one person, a reading corner or a bedroom. A recliner sofa puts two or three seats in one piece. On some sofas every seat reclines. On others only the end seats do, and the middle stays fixed. A full set, such as a three-seater with matching single chairs, suits a family living room or a home theatre.

Some designs add cup-holder arms and padded headrests. Before you order, check which seats recline. Also check that each seat can open fully without hitting a wall, a table or the next seat.

## Which upholstery suits a recliner?

Choose upholstery that can take more rubbing than a normal sofa, because a recliner moves each time it opens. Some fabric suppliers suggest 25,000 rubs or more on the Martindale test for recliners.

- **Fabric** breathes, so it stays comfortable through Pune's hot months. Plush fabrics feel soft and come in many colours.
- **Leatherette** wipes clean easily. Low-grade leatherette can crack or peel over time, and faster in heat, damp and strong sun. Ask about its quality.
- **Velvet** feels rich, but it can show patches where the pile gets pressed.

Fabric, leatherette and colour choices depend on the model, so confirm yours in the quotation.

## How to look after a recliner

A recliner lasts longer when you use it gently and keep its moving parts clear.

- Return the chair upright when you get up. Never sit or stand on the footrest.
- Keep children, pets and toys away from the mechanism while it moves.
- Check under the chair now and then for anything caught in the moving parts.
- Keep power cables away from the mechanism and out of walkways.
- Vacuum fabric gently every week. Ask the maker for cleaning advice for your fabric.
- Wipe leatherette with a soft, slightly damp cloth, then dry it.
- In the monsoon, keep the recliner away from damp walls and open windows.

## What to ask for in your quotation

Ask for these points in writing:

- The mechanism: push-back, lever, power or power-lift
- Which seats recline, on a recliner sofa
- The space needed behind the chair, and its full open length
- Battery backup, for power and power-lift models
- The upholstery name, colour, and rub count if listed
- The frame and foam, as for any sofa
- Whether the back lifts off for delivery
- Delivery and fitting: what is included, and whether your area is covered
- Any warranty on the mechanism and motor, and what it covers

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between a recliner and a power recliner?
A manual recliner opens when you lean back or pull a lever. A power recliner uses a motor instead. You press a button, and it moves smoothly and can stop at any angle. Power suits anyone who finds a lever hard to pull. It needs a socket nearby, and a battery backup helps during power cuts.

### How far should a recliner be from the wall?
A standard recliner often needs 25 to 60 cm behind it. A wall-hugger, or zero-wall, recliner often needs 15 cm or less, because its seat slides forward as it reclines. Figures differ by model. Open the chair fully in the showroom and measure. Then leave room in front for the footrest and a walkway.

### Which recliner is best for sleeping?
A recliner is for resting, not a full replacement for a bed. For naps, choose one that reclines far, supports your head and lower back, and lets you change position easily. A power recliner lets you adjust it without effort. If you need to sleep upright for a health reason, ask your doctor first.

### Which recliners last the longest?
The ones with a strong frame, a well-made steel mechanism, dense seat foam and hard-wearing upholstery. Gentle use matters just as much. Return the chair upright before standing, keep the mechanism clear, and do not let children ride on the footrest. Ask what the mechanism is made of, and what is covered if it fails.

### Is a recliner good for working from home?
A recliner is good for breaks, not for long hours at a laptop. It is hard to keep a good posture while typing in a reclined seat. For work, use a proper desk and chair. Then move to the recliner to rest your back and legs between calls, or at the end of the day.

### How do I set up a recliner in the living room?
Place it where it can open fully without hitting a wall, a table or a door. Leave a clear path past the open footrest. Face it towards the TV or a window at a comfortable angle. For a power recliner, keep it near a socket, and run the cable away from the mechanism and the walkway.
